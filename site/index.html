Three desk statuses. Each is a single rule applied to what the detector found in the frame.

Someone is in the seat and there is no phone in their hands. It says the person is present. It says nothing about their output.

How it works

Four steps from footage to a log you can review. Nothing is installed on the cameras and nothing is labelled by hand.

  1. Point it at footage

    Give it a recorded clip or a live camera feed from the cameras you already have.

  2. It maps the floor

    In the first second it finds everyone who is seated and marks each seat. For doors, shelves and driveways, you draw the zones.

  3. It watches for change

    A detector finds people, phones, vehicles and pets in every frame, and a pose model finds which way each head is turned. Fixed rules turn those detections into statuses.

  4. You review

    Each change lands in a timestamped log. Jump to the moment, mark it reviewed, export it. Nothing is acted on automatically.

Where it works

One engine covers five kinds of floor. You draw a zone on the camera view, pick a rule and set its threshold.

Offices

Desk occupancy
Which seats are filled right now, and which have emptied.
Phone in hand
A phone being held, as opposed to one lying on the desk.
Walk-throughs
People crossing the floor are counted separately from people at desks.

Shop floors

Restricted areas
Someone stepping into a stockroom or staff-only doorway.
Dwell at a display
A person standing at a shelf or cabinet past a time you set.
Queue length
More people at the checkout than your limit, held for a moment.

Exam halls

Head direction
How far each candidate's head has turned from where it started, in degrees.
Whose desk
Which desk the head is pointing at, and for how long.
Phones
A phone at a desk raises a flag for an invigilator to check.

Industrial sites

Exclusion zones
A person inside the marked area around machinery.
Corridor occupancy
Too many people in a walkway at the same time.
Movement heatmaps
Where people spent time across the footage, without tracking who.

Homes

Front door
A person on the path to the door raises an alert within half a second.
Driveway
A vehicle pulling in is logged as a notice and raises no alarm.
Pets
Dogs and cats are recognised and left out of the alerts.

These are the nano models, chosen so they load quickly. The demo clips above were analysed with the large ones, which find smaller objects such as phones and place keypoints more steadily.

Models
YOLO26 from Ultralytics. Detection comes in three sizes: nano (9.9 MB) for live use and this page, small (38 MB) when accuracy matters more than speed, large for offline analysis. YOLO26 pose adds 17 body keypoints per person.
Runtime
ONNX Runtime. Native on your server, with an NVIDIA GPU if you have one, and WebAssembly inside this page.
Speed, measured
68 ms a frame for nano and 208 ms for small at 640 px, on a 2-core Xeon cloud CPU with no GPU. Large at 1280 px takes about 2 seconds a frame on the same machine.
The demo run
964 frames analysed with the large models. 7,068 detections kept above 25% confidence, 41,801 body keypoints and 13 status changes logged.
Rules
Zone entry, dwell, occupancy limits, seat absence, phone in hand, head turn and gaze target. 41 automated tests cover them.

Deployment

Sentinel Vision is software you run on your own machine. There is no cloud service behind it and no account to create.

Runs on
Your own computer or server. Footage is processed there and is not uploaded.
Identity
None. There is no face recognition, no names and no demographic guesses. Head direction comes from five keypoints on the head. Each person gets a number for the session, and it is dropped when they leave the frame.
Detects
People, phones, vehicles, dogs and cats. Where head direction matters, 17 body keypoints per person.
Input
Recorded video files and live camera feeds.
Output
Timestamped events as CSV and JSON, annotated video and movement heatmaps.
Retention
Generated clips and reports are deleted after 24 hours by default. You can shorten or extend that.
Decisions
None are automated. Every status is a prompt for a person to look.
Rules
Fixed and inspectable. Each alert states the zone, the threshold and the time it fired.

Responsible use

Sentinel Vision reports what a camera can see: presence, position and objects. It cannot see intent or effort.

"Working" means someone is at their desk. "Phone usage" means a phone is in their hands. Neither says why, and neither should be acted on without a person looking at the footage first.

In an exam hall, a flag means a head stayed turned toward another desk, or a phone appeared at a desk. It gives an invigilator a timestamp to check. Whether anything improper happened is for them to decide.

Tell the people in frame that the system is running and what it records, and follow the workplace-monitoring and privacy rules that apply where you operate.
